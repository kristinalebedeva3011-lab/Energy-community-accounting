**Assessing electricity sharing in energy communities using real consumption and simulated generation data**\

**Abstract**

Buildings account for a significant share of energy demand and CO2 emissions, motivating microgrid solutions to support global clean energy (SDG 7) and climate action (SDG 13) goals. Energy communities with distributed photovoltaic (PV) generation and peer-to-peer (P2P) electricity sharing offer a promising approach, yet their real-world performance in multi-apartment building contexts remains under-examined. This study quantifies the operation of a PV-based energy community in an urban microdistrict (20 diverse buildings, mostly 4–9-story multi-apartment residential) to evaluate local electricity sharing, consumption dynamics, and emission reductions. Real hourly electricity consumption profiles and building characteristics were analyzed as inputs. Dynamic PV generation was simulated using PolySun SPT software for each rooftop. A custom Python-based modeling framework captured hourly generation–consumption interactions and enabled energy sharing among buildings. From April to September, PV systems produce ~78% (892.19 MWh) of annual output. During these months, local generation meets most daytime demand, with centralized grid imports needed primarily at night. Over the year, direct self-consumption covers 22% (431.28 MWh) of total demand, while sharing supplies an additional 26% (156.56 MWh) by reallocating surplus generation. Active electricity sharing within communities reduces dependence on external grid imports by another ~10% (207.62 MWh). The combined PV and sharing scenario cuts CO2 emissions by approximately 60% compared to a baseline without these measures. However, in winter the community remains a net consumer, underlining seasonal shortfalls. Internal electricity sharing significantly enhances local renewable utilization and emissions savings, illustrating the potential of community microgrids for sustainable urban energy systems. Nonetheless, long-term autonomy will require integrating energy storage and complementary renewables to address winter deficits. The presented modeling framework provides a replicable tool for future research and policy design.


**Keywords**: electricity sharing, photovoltaic (PV), rooftop PV system, energy community, self-consumption
